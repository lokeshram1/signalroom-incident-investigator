01 / choose your problem

Three problems I keep seeing.

These are grounded in work with data and AI systems. The first two are observed patterns; the third is a plausible opportunity I would validate.

01chosen

Incident investigation is scattered.

Where: In production support and IncidentIQ-style systems I’ve built. Who: on-call engineers, SREs, and incident leads.

What happens: logs, dashboards, deploy notes, and tribal knowledge live in different places. People form a hypothesis too early, lose the trail, and spend the first 30 minutes reconstructing context instead of reducing impact.

02

Customer data arrives before its meaning.

Where: Data onboarding projects. Who: analytics engineers and operations teams receiving CSV exports.

What happens: every new file becomes a manual mapping exercise. Bad dates, duplicate IDs, and renamed columns slip downstream, creating rework and low trust in reporting.

03

Small teams cannot see pricing risk early.

Where: E-commerce operations. Who: category managers and marketplace analysts.

What happens: reviews happen after a customer notices an incorrect price. A useful detector could prioritize a small queue, but the value and acceptable false-positive rate still need validation.

Why this one first

It has a sharp first step and a measurable cost.

Observed: investigation work is fragmented and repetitive. Assumed: a ranked evidence view will reduce time-to-first-useful-action without encouraging blind trust in AI. I would explore this ahead of the other two because the user is already under time pressure, the inputs are available, and a narrow prototype can prove or disprove the workflow quickly.

02 / develop the approach

Make the reasoning inspectable.

TODAY

People correlate by hand

They jump between logs, metrics, deploy history, and chat. The best investigators build a mental timeline; newer responders often treat the loudest error as the cause.

→
BETTER OUTCOME

One evidence-linked brief

Give responders a timeline, competing hypotheses, supporting and contradicting signals, and a next action. AI can summarize and rank; deterministic rules should keep the evidence honest.

→
SMALLEST FIRST STEP

Replay one incident packet

Start with structured text: symptoms, log lines, metrics, and recent deploys. Prove that the system can produce a useful brief before adding live integrations.

Alternative considered

Ask an LLM to read everything and explain.

I did not choose a free-form chat interface first. It is easy to demo but hard to audit: a confident explanation can hide missing evidence. A structured brief makes the core value testable and gives a future chat layer something reliable to work from.

Need to learn

Which evidence types change a responder’s decision? What confidence language prevents over-trust? Can we reduce time-to-first-action in a replay study?

Stop signal

If responders do not act faster, cannot explain why a hypothesis ranked first, or reject the evidence links as noisy, the problem is not worth expanding.
